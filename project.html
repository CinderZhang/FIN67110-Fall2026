The project

Your team builds, with AI, an intelligent finance product a firm could adopt, not a standard app. The grade reads the product. Everything on this page is a way a reader checks it without taking your word: a stranger runs it, it ran live, its numbers hold against the filings, the product manual claims no more than the evidence shows, and each of you can defend it. Due date: the schedule. Points and tiers: grading.

The question, the same for everyone

Can an AI research desk your team built read any company's filings and its earnings release correctly, serve one client's decision, and prove it live?

Teams

Three students. If the roster does not divide by three, the instructor forms one or two teams of two. Teams form by the end of Session 5 (the schedule): choose freely, and the instructor places anyone unplaced at the end of that session. The team list is posted in Brightspace → Course data after Session 5. A team that loses a member keeps every obligation, and each remaining member answers a question. How the team works together, and where it keeps its files, is the team's business.

The client

The client is an investor seeking absolute return, taught in Week 4. The desk's last skill produces a view on the report, worth more or less than the price, with the evidence; the product manual compares the views against what the price already said. A team may serve an institution instead: it builds the mandate skill described on the Week 5 page as the thing it owns, and its product manual compares relative return net of fee against the benchmark the mandate names. The parts, the ranking and the calendar are the same for every team.

What you submit: five parts, one Brightspace upload

The five parts, like the checkpoints before them, are not assignments. They are examples of how the product is inspected, and the reader may inspect it any way the product allows. Upload one Markdown file to Project that links to parts 1, 3 and 4 on github.com, wherever your team keeps them, and names your session from the posted order. Use permanent links (github.com's "Copy permalink" on a file), which freeze each file as it was when you submitted. Its shape:

  1. The desk. The code for the four roles with their tools, the verifier, the closes reader and the client's skill. It runs on any U.S. company that files with EDGAR, from its ticker, reading the filings and the earnings release itself. A name the seeker never saw runs too, with the spinoff parts "not applicable." The repository's README is enough for a team that is not yours to run it; another team does, in Session 10.
  2. The live record. Already in Brightspace. Nothing to resubmit. The rule is below.
  3. The audits. The report audit of two of your own live reports and of the two reports your own run of another team's desk produced, from its README alone, on two companies you chose (the exchange in Session 10), each against the filings the desk read: four tables, with the error and missing counts at the top of each. Above the other team's two tables, one line: the command you ran, the date, and whether you needed that team's help. That line is the other team's ready check, and with your two tables it is their adoption evidence, so write it as it was. List your own two first; the reader checks the first one and may check any of the four.
  4. The product manual, in Markdown: a client reads it in one sitting, runs the desk from it, and judges its record from it. The four tables and the sentences they need: - the question and the client; - how the desk works, with one diagram of the agent team, the verifier, the tools, the skills, the hook and the one command, and which join it uses where (Week 5); - the live record: a table of every report, its release, its filing time, its upload time, and whether it was inside the window; - the audits: both counts for all four reports, and why you chose the two of your own; - what the desk told the client, with the hit-rate table and the base rate under it, what happened afterward, and what four weeks can and cannot show. Conclusions cover the reports you audited and the desk you built, nothing wider; - the decision log: what your AI agents got wrong, how you caught it, and what you would not delegate again, and what the attacks found if your team ran them, with the three-column table of what the join changed (Week 5). Shape, not your answer: "Our Financial Analyst read the non-GAAP EPS as GAAP on the first live report. The verifier marked it unsupported because the quoted line was not in the release. We now have both figures reported, each labeled." - the roles: one paragraph per member, their role and contribution, written by that member (the Week 7 shape).
  5. The presentation, eleven minutes, in Session 13 or 14 (the order is posted in Brightspace), then three minutes of questions, one per member, from the instructor or the room, on a number shown in the talk. Inside the eleven minutes, the instructor puts on the screen a U.S. company that filed an earnings release on EDGAR in the past 30 days, with its ticker, its Exhibit 99.1 URL and its "Accepted" time, and the team runs its desk on that ticker, live, on its own laptop. One retry if the network or a model provider fails.

The live record

The rule in one line: a report every week in Weeks 4 to 7; one missed week costs nothing; two missed weeks keep the team out of the top tier; no report ever is the fail rule. Week 6, Thanksgiving week, is the week most teams will miss, and that is fine.

A week runs Monday to Sunday; the schedule gives the dates of Weeks 4 to 7. In each, your team's desk runs on at least one company whose earnings release was filed that week, and the report goes to Brightspace → Live record within 24 hours of the release's filing time on EDGAR, the "Accepted" time on the filing's index page (your Lab 02 steps print it). The report names the filings it read, that release among them. Which companies report that week: start here, the Nasdaq earnings calendar, then find the release on EDGAR (8-K, Exhibit 99.1). More reports are welcome and never required. Brightspace keeps every submission with its timestamp: that is the record that your desk read a real release in real time. Week 8 has no live obligation; Brightspace outages are on the grading page.

The peer evaluation

After the presentations, each member evaluates their teammates alone, in Brightspace → Peer evaluation. The rule, the score and the deadline are on the grading page. The roles section of the product manual is the team's shared statement; the peer evaluation is shared with nobody.

How it is ranked

First, the fail rule on the grading page is checked. A project that passes it but misses one of the five parts is in the submitted tier. Then the ready check: another team ran your desk from its README alone, on its own machine, on companies it chose, and the run produced a report. The line above that team's audit tables says so, and the reader may rerun the command. A desk that needed your help to run, or did not run, is complete but not ready, and is in the submitted tier. Every other project is read. Two gates decide who can reach the top tier: "ran live" and "adopted". The others are ordered by the remaining four questions in sequence: each question sorts the teams into those that meet it and those that do not, and the next question orders within each group.

Criterion The question the reader asks
Ran live (a gate) A report inside its 24-hour window in at least three of Weeks 4 to 7, each naming the release it read; the Brightspace timestamp against the EDGAR "Accepted" time decides, or the email's timestamp under the outage rule on the grading page.
Adopted (a gate) Another team ran your desk from its README alone, on companies it chose, and its audit of the two reports that run produced found no wrong claim in either table: the missing counts may be above zero, the error counts may not. The reader may check that team's tables against the filings. A reader outside the course who ran your desk from its README alone and wrote what they used it for counts the same way, their writing linked from the product manual in place of the second team's tables; welcome, never required.
Audits hold The reader checks the first of your own audit tables against the filings, and may check any other. One wrong mark is a slip. Two or more wrong marks in one table fail this question.
The desk is general It ran on the instructor's pick in the presentation, the report was about that company, and one of its core claims, spot-checked by the reader, carried a line the filing contains.
Honest reading Does the product manual say what the live record, the audits and the client comparison support, including nothing, and no more?
Each member can explain it Did every member present answer their question on a number shown in the talk?

The top tier is at least the top third of the projects that pass the fail rule, rounded up (for example 4 of 10). If fewer projects than that pass the two gates, only those that pass are in the top tier. Ranking waits for any part extended by an excused absence. Teams tied with the last team inside the top third after the four questions are all placed in the top tier, even where that exceeds a third. How your desk's calls turned out, in returns or in beats, is never a criterion. A desk that was wrong for its client every week, reported honestly, can rank at the top.
