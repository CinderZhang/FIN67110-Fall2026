The project

One desk, one client, one live season, one team. Due date: the schedule. Points and tiers: grading.

The question, the same for everyone

Can an AI research desk your team built read any company's filings and its earnings release correctly, serve one client's decision, and prove it live?

Teams

Three students; with 29 enrolled, one team of two. Teams form by the end of Session 5 (the schedule): choose freely, and the instructor places anyone unplaced at the end of that session. The team list is posted in Brightspace → Course data after Session 5. A team that loses a member keeps every obligation, and each remaining member answers a question. How the team works together, and where it keeps its files, is the team's business.

The client

Two clients are taught to everyone, in Week 4 and Week 5. Your team names one at Checkpoint 3. The parts, the ranking and the calendar are the same for every team. The client changes only the desk's last layer and what its output is compared against.

Client What the desk's last layer produces Compared against
An investor seeking absolute return A view on the report: worth more or less than the price, with the evidence What the price already said, as taught in Week 4
An institution with a mandate A position inside the mandate's limits, net of the fee it charges The benchmark the mandate names, as taught in Week 5

The live record

The rule in one line: a report every week in Weeks 4 to 7; one missed week costs nothing; two missed weeks keep the team out of the top tier; no report ever is the fail rule. Week 6, Thanksgiving week, is the week most teams will miss, and that is fine.

A week runs Monday to Sunday; the schedule gives the dates of Weeks 4 to 7. In each, your team's desk runs on at least one company whose earnings release was filed that week, and the report goes to Brightspace → Live record within 24 hours of the release's filing time on EDGAR, the "Accepted" time on the filing's index page (the Week 3 page shows one). The report names the filings it read, that release among them. Which companies report that week: start here, the Nasdaq earnings calendar, then find the release on EDGAR (8-K, Exhibit 99.1). More reports are welcome and never required. Brightspace keeps every submission with its timestamp: that is the record that your desk read a real release in real time. Week 8 has no live obligation; Brightspace outages are on the grading page.

How it is ranked

First, the fail rule on the grading page is checked. Every other project is read. "Ran live" is a gate: a team with a report inside its window in fewer than three of the four weeks cannot reach the top tier. The others are ordered by the remaining four questions in sequence: each question sorts the teams into those that meet it and those that do not, and the next question orders within each group.

Criterion The question the reader asks
Ran live (the gate) A report inside its 24-hour window in at least three of Weeks 4 to 7, each naming the release it read; the Brightspace timestamp against the EDGAR "Accepted" time decides.
Audits hold The reader checks the first of your own audit tables against the filings, and may check any other. One wrong mark is a slip. Two or more wrong marks in one table fail this question.
The desk is general It ran on the instructor's pick in the presentation, the report was about that company, and one of its core claims, spot-checked by the reader, carried a line the filing contains.
Honest reading Does the paper say what the live record, the audits and the client comparison support, including nothing, and no more?
Each member can explain it Did every member present answer their question on a number shown in the talk?

The top tier is at least the top third of the projects that pass the fail rule, rounded up (for example 4 of 10). If fewer projects than that pass the gate, only those that pass are in the top tier. Ranking waits for any part extended by an excused absence. Teams still level after the four questions are all placed in the top tier, even where that exceeds a third. How your desk's calls turned out, in returns or in beats, is never a criterion. A desk that was wrong for its client every week, reported honestly, can rank at the top.
