Lab 07 — Your Investor

One thing today: work one name from your screen through the worksheet, with a hook that checks every number, make the investor your team's own, and file the first live report.

You arrive with: Tuesday's seeker running; the worksheet file and the persona written by hand; the live release your team chose; your model's cutoff recorded; the thing your team decided to own.

Open — the paired review (10 minutes)

  1. Tools alive. The seeker runs; the agent team runs on a ticker.
  2. Defend. Show your partner the top of your screen. Partner asks what the rank does not know.
  3. Gap check. Write one line: what makes a hit rate of seven in ten meaningless?

D — the question, the same for everyone

Can the desk judge one name the seeker found, with every number traced to a filing, a value range against the price, and a view the live record can score?

R — what you work from

Input Where
One name from your screen your partner picks it from your report, not you
Its filings the Form 10 and its information statement, the first 10-Q or 10-K, the Form 4s, on EDGAR
Closes and volumes your closes reader, plus a volume column
The worksheet's parts Week 4 page, Teaching 4; your own version, written by hand
This week's live release the one your team chose from the earnings calendar

I — the worksheet, the hook, then make it yours

  1. Send your coding agent the request:

Make a second skill, named for the worksheet. It runs on a saved run of my agent team for one ticker and on the filings the run saved. When the ticker is in my seeker's registry, it also fetches the Form 10 and its information statement from EDGAR by the registry's accession and saves them with the run; otherwise the spinoff parts print "not applicable" and the rest runs. In code, it fills the facts of the worksheet from the filings, one line per fact with an amount, and "not known" where the filing states none, never a list of sentences: the parent, the ratio and the dates from the information statement; the debt raised and where the cash went; the liabilities and the restrictions the parent left, each with the filing and the section; insiders' purchases from Form 4, code P only; and the median daily dollar volume from the closes and volumes my reader supplies, against the client's purchase size in my worksheet file. Mark each figure historical, pro forma, or reported after the spin, by the statement it came from. Compute owner cash flow as after-tax earnings plus depreciation minus capital spending, from the statements, with the arithmetic printed, and a value range, low and mid, by the method written in my worksheet file, with the rates stated, and print the price against the low case. Then make one model call, with the persona file I wrote, to write the judgment from the filing facts, the value range and the price against the low case, and nothing else from the reader: why the name may be cheap or why it is not that story, and what in the next filing would change the view. Add the liquidity line after the call, in code. End with a view on the release, more or less than the price, with the one number that decides it and its filing or line; a conviction, high or low, by the rule in my worksheet file; and a "not known" list of what it could not find. When the close is "not available," the price lines and the view print "not computed." Save the worksheet with the run, dated. No model computes a number. Nothing in the code names a company. Tell me the exact command to run it on a saved run.

Rewrite it in ASD-STE100 yourself before you send it, as Week 1 taught; a sample is here.

  1. Run the agent team on the name your partner picked, then the worksheet, then put the worksheet on your page beside the report. Expect: every fact with a filing and a section; owner cash flow with its arithmetic; a low and a mid case; the price against the low case; a judgment that ends with what would change it; the view with one number; the "not known" list. The shape, not your answer: debt raised: [amount]; [filing], [section]; cash to the parent: [amount]; pro forma.
  2. Send the hook request:

Add a hook to my agent that runs when a skill has written its output. The hook runs a script that reads every number in the saved worksheet itself, not in a file written for the hook. A number that claims a tag and a period is looked up in the run's facts file, exactly; a number that claims a filing and a line is found in that filing's saved text; a number computed from printed inputs is recomputed from them; a rate or a weight is matched against the skill's file; a price is matched against the reader's output. A date, an accession number or a line number is an identifier, not a claim, and is skipped. A number of no such kind is a failure. On any failure the hook refuses "done" and prints the line that failed. Show me the hook's file before you enable it. Tell me the exact command to test the hook on a saved run.

Rewrite it in ASD-STE100 yourself before you send it, as Week 1 taught; a sample is here.

  1. Read the hook's file: it runs as you. Enable it.
  2. Seed one error. Copy the worksheet, change one amount by one, and run the hook on the copy. Expect: a refusal that names the line. Run it on the clean worksheet. Expect: silence. Then, with the hook enabled, tell your agent the seeded copy is the skill's output and ask it to finish. Expect: it does not say done. The shape, not your answer: refused: line 9, [amount] is not in [filing], [section].
  3. Own the investor. As a team, start the thing you decided on, at whatever depth today reaches, and write it in one line in the README. Three worth owning, from the Week 4 page's levels, or one of your own: the second seeker, for stocks below net current asset value, with the book's exclusions as code and the haircuts shown; the factor check, fixed in a file before any return is seen, honest about how many comparisons it ran; the plugin, with evals that run the same question with and without the skill.
  4. The hit-rate table. Ask your agent for a command that reads every run in a folder you name, fills in any reaction that has become available since the run without touching the view or its time, and prints one row per run: ticker, filing time, the view's time, direction, reaction, agree or disagree, and "after" when the view's time is later than the first close after the filing time. The last line counts agreements over runs with a reaction, leaving out "after" rows and views "not computed". Live runs go in runs/live, practice runs elsewhere.
  5. The live report. If this week's release has filed, run the agent team and the worksheet skill on it now, into runs/live, and upload the verified report with the skill's output under it to Brightspace → Live record before its window closes; the product manual scores the view in that upload. If it files later this week, run it then.
  6. While it runs: one item from your gap list, in a second AI chat.

Base rates — Learn on your own

  1. What is a base rate (what a coin would score on your hit-rate table)?
  2. How does it work here (half the directions agree by chance; a one-day move does not score the value range)?
  3. Explain it: how many live reports would your team need before seven in ten said anything?

Reflect

Explain to your partner: 1. the thing your team owns, and what it costs you; 2. the line of the worksheet you trust least, and why.
