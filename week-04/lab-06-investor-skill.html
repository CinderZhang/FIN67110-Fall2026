Lab 06 — The Seeker

One thing today: turn a book's chapter into a skill your agent runs: a spinoff seeker that finds U.S. spinoffs on EDGAR, prices them with your own code, and ranks a screen by factors you declared. (45 minutes, after the teaching.)

You arrive with: your agent team running; your closes reader printing a close; your factor list written by hand, with the direction each factor should point and the chapter it came from; one recent Form 10 read; your agent's skills and hooks pages read.

Open — the paired review (10 minutes)

  1. Tools alive. The closes reader prints the last close before a time; your agent lists its skills, the minimal one you made among them.
  2. Defend. Read your partner your factor list. Partner asks: which factor would the book's author strike, and why?
  3. Gap check. Write the filing sequence of a spinoff from memory: the forms, in order, from the announcement to the first Form 4. If you cannot, that is today's gap.

D — the question, the same for everyone

Can a chapter about who sells and why become a procedure your agent runs on EDGAR every week, with every name's facts traced to a filing and every rank explained?

R — what you work from

Input Where
The filing sequence of a spinoff, and what each form holds Week 4 page, Teaching 2
The filings SEC EDGAR: the daily and full indexes, the submissions API, the filing folders, and the full-text search (the data page)
Prices your closes reader
Your factors, with their directions the list you wrote before class, from the chapters

I — build it from the contract

What follows is the contract: what the seeker must do and what it must never do. The design is yours: the folder, the factors and their weights, the window, the shape of the report, the name. Send it as it is or in your own words, and keep every sentence that says "never."

  1. Send your coding agent the request:

Make a skill for my coding agent, in the folder where my agent reads skills, and name it for what it seeks. It finds U.S. spinoffs on SEC EDGAR whose distribution date is on or after a start date I give. It reads EDGAR's form indexes for Form 10 registrations, their amendments, the exchanges' CERT filings and the 8-Ks filed at a distribution, and it reads each filing's folder on EDGAR for the facts it needs, sending my SEC User-Agent from .env on every request and staying under the rate EDGAR allows. It keeps a registry file of facts it established by reading a filing: the parent, the distribution ratio, the record and distribution dates, each with the filing and the section it came from. It fills the registry from the filings it can read; a fact it cannot find stays blank, and the report says so. It never estimates a fact. It drops a registrant that is not a spinoff, with the reason and the filing beside it: a Form 10 with no information statement, a fund, or a company registering shares it already has is dropped, unless a later filing shows a parent distributing the business. It gives each name a stage: registered, listing approved, trading, in its window, or past it. The distribution 8-K is often the parent's, so the skill reads the parent's filings from the registry's parent CIK; a 10-Q, a 10-K or a Form 4 in the new company's own filing list proves it trades. A name's first price is the first close after the distribution date, never a when-issued price. It takes closes from my closes reader and prints the price date beside every price. It ranks the names in their window by the factors in a file I write, each with its weight and direction, and the same file states the window's length in days after the distribution; it prints each name's points per factor, and a name missing a date it needs is listed unranked, with the reason. It writes a report with the run date, the price date and the index date on the first line, the names in their window by rank, the watch list of registered names not yet trading, what was dropped and why, what the data cannot show, and the line "screens, not recommendations" at the end, saved with the date and time it ran. Every rule has a test on made-up data, and a rule changes only after its test does. Nothing in the code names a company. Tell me the exact command to run the seeker from a start date and the exact command to run its tests.

The same request, written to the standard: requests.

  1. Run the tests, then the seeker, from a start date about a year back, then put the screen on your page: a start date in, the report out. Expect: the report with its three dates on the first line; names in their window with points per factor; a watch list; a dropped list with a reason and a filing beside each; the last line. The shape of one row, not your answer: [name] | [parent] | distributed [date] | window ends [date] | [price] on [price date] | [points]: size [n], weakness [n], insiders [n].
  2. Write one factor's weight to zero in your factors file and run again on the same inputs. Expect: that factor's points disappear from every name; the other factors' points do not change; the ranks may.
  3. While it runs: work one item from your gap list in a second AI chat. Never collected.
  4. Something not working? Debug with your AI: paste the exact command and the exact error text.

Skills with scripts — Learn on your own

  1. What is a skill (a folder your agent loads when a task matches its description, with scripts beside the instructions), and why does a seeker need scripts and not only instructions?
  2. How does it work here: which lines of your report come from code, and which would a reader still have to check in the filing?
  3. Explain it: open the first screen of FAskills. What does its processing layer compute before any persona sees the data, and what is the equivalent in your seeker?

Reflect

Explain to your partner: 1. the name at the top of your screen, and the one line from the chapter that put it there; 2. the fact in your registry you trust least, and why.
