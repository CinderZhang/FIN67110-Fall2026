Grading and course policies — FIN 67110, Fall 2026

The one place for points and policies. Dates are on the schedule.

Points

Every component is scored out of 100. The course total is the weighted sum below.

Component Weight What 100 means
Checkpoint 1 — your desk runs 17.5% By 9:30 AM, the end of Session 3: in Brightspace Checkpoint 1, a permalink (a commit link, as the project page shows) to your repository holding your desk and a report on a company your partner chose; a report from the fallback on the Week 2 page counts
Checkpoint 2 — the verifier 17.5% By 9:30 AM, the end of Session 5: in Checkpoint 2, a permalink to your repository holding the verifier and its marks on the seeded-error test (the known answer on the Lab 04 page)
Checkpoint 3 — the team proposal 17.5% By 9:30 AM, the end of Session 9: one Markdown page in Checkpoint 3 naming the team, its client, the design of the desk's client layer, and the live record so far
Checkpoint 4 — the draft 17.5% By 9:30 AM, the end of Session 12: the team's draft paper in Checkpoint 4 with the live-record table, the audit table and the client paragraph
Project 30% Five parts, including the team presentation; ranked once, at the end: see below

A checkpoint scores 100 (complete) or 0 (incomplete), never in between. Checkpoints 1 and 2 are individual. Checkpoints 3 and 4 are one upload per team, and every member receives the same score. The lab page for each checkpoint shows the same requirement at the spot where you do it. The Session 1 checkout records completion and carries no weight.

The project, ranked once

Tier Project score Who
Top 95 About the top third of projects that pass the fail rule
Standard 85 Every other project that passes the fail rule
Fail 0 No note of your team's ever reached Brightspace Live record, or your team submitted no paper

The fail rule is checked first. Complete means all five parts on the project page are submitted. A project that passes the fail rule but misses a part is ranked in the standard tier. Ranking uses only the five published criteria on the project page. The tier is the team's. How your desk's calls turned out, in returns or in beats, is never a criterion.

With all four checkpoints complete, the top tier's course total is 0.3 × 95 + 70 = 98.5 (A) and the standard tier's is 0.3 × 85 + 70 = 95.5 (A). Each missed checkpoint costs 17.5 points.

Absences and outages

  • Checkpoint sessions: a university-excused absence (Dean of Students documentation) extends that checkpoint's upload by 48 hours. A team checkpoint is unaffected by one member's absence.
  • The live record: the rule is on the project page. If Brightspace is down inside a window, email the report to the instructor before it closes; the email's timestamp counts.
  • Your presentation: with a university-excused absence, answer one question from the instructor, in writing, within 48 hours.
  • Brightspace or GitHub down at a checkpoint deadline: email the instructor before the deadline with everything the upload would hold. The email's timestamp counts.
  • Your own setup failing is not an outage. See below. If your desk, or your model provider, is down during a checkpoint session, the fallback on the Week 2 page produces the report, and that report counts for Checkpoint 1 and the live record. Checkpoint 2 needs the verifier's marks on the seeded test, which can be produced before the session; a provider outage during Session 5 extends that upload by 48 hours.
  • Approved accommodations through the Disability Resource Center apply as written in your letter.

Prerequisites — your setup is yours

Come to Session 1 with these working. Setting them up and fixing them is your responsibility, with your AI's help, and technical problems with your own setup are not grounds for an extension. In Session 1 your partner checks each one with you, and anything still failing must work by Session 2, when your desk is built. To start, ask any AI chat: "Walk me through installing Python 3.11 and [the agent you chose] on my [Windows or Mac] laptop, one step at a time."

  1. A laptop with a current Python 3 that runs a program from a terminal. Check: python --version (on Windows, if python is not found, try py --version).
  2. Your AI agents and Orca. Required: OpenAI's Codex CLI, Google Antigravity with its agy command line, and Orca, which runs your agents side by side on your work folder. Claude Code is optional. Install each from its own page. A browser chat is not a coding agent: it cannot create and run files in your folder. Two agents because having a second model check the first is this course's verification lesson applied to your own build.
  3. An API key with one model provider, created in the provider's developer console: for example OpenAI, Anthropic or Google AI Studio. Your desk calls a model through that key. A chat subscription does not include one.
  4. A public GitHub repository for your work. Nobody is invited: the instructor, and the teaching assistant if one is assigned, read it as anyone can. Put files there with git push, or on github.com with Add file → Upload files. One warning: the repository is public, so a key pushed there is a key stolen. Dragging a whole folder onto github.com bypasses .gitignore and would publish your .env.
  5. Accounts with the data Purdue licenses: LSEG Workspace, WRDS and S&P Capital IQ, each registered with your Purdue email as the data page describes. Register in Week 1: LSEG's approval takes days, and you first need the data in Week 3.

Keep every key out of GitHub: Lab 01 has your AI create a .env file with placeholders, tell you what to paste where, and exclude it with .gitignore. Check github.com afterward that .env is not there.

AI use

AI is expected in every part of this course. You are responsible for every number you submit: if your AI wrote it, you checked it. Anything you make public ends with these three lines:

This is a learning exercise, not financial advice. AI assistance: [the tools you used]. I verified the sources myself; the judgments are mine. Any remaining errors are my own.

Integrity

Purdue's academic integrity policy applies. Your desk, reports and audits before teams form are your own work; within a team, the project is shared work and every member can explain every part of it. One thing the policy might make you hesitate over is required: the audit of another team's reports, credited to its authors. Classmates may help you debug.

The data you use

Public filings and XBRL data from SEC EDGAR, LSEG Workspace and WRDS through Purdue, and whatever else your own code can reach. The data page is the contract: what the reader returns, the known answers, and the one rule.

Class data and privacy

Repositories are public, and yours holds your audits of another team's reports, credited to its authors, because the project requires them there. Beyond that, do not repost another team's reports or audits, and in anything you write for the public outside your repository, report only your own team's results. The presentations are class-only.

Course communication

Questions about the material go to class or office hours. Your setup and your code are yours to debug with your AI. Grades are never discussed by email.

University policies

Purdue's policies on academic integrity, accessibility and accommodations, nondiscrimination, emergency procedures, and mental health resources apply and are listed in the course syllabus, posted in Brightspace → Syllabus.
