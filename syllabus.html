Teaching Philosophy

Every lab runs on DRIVER. Its shape is D · [R-I-V-E] · R: you own the edges, your AI works the middle, and you stay on the loop. Discover and define the question before any AI sees it. Represent the approach. Implement with your AI. Validate against a known answer. Evolve what you built into something reusable. Reflect by explaining it to a person without the screen: if you cannot, you do not understand it yet. The method in one essay: The Middle Is for the Machine. The Edges Are for You. (Zhang, 2026).

How to Succeed in this Course

  • You build the desk yourself, with your AI coding agent, from the paper's flowchart. No code is handed out.
  • Every tool you build runs on any company. Companies are test inputs: in class your partner picks the release, and during the season your team's desk runs on whatever reports that week.
  • Tuesday is half teaching and half lab on the training case, Costco, where the answers are known. Thursday is a full lab: you make the same capability your own and prove it on a release you did not choose.
  • From Week 4 the desk serves a client: first an investor who wants absolute return, then an institution that must beat a benchmark under a mandate. Your team builds for one of them.
  • One team project answers the question with your desk, its live record and its audits.
  • You use AI to learn and to discover. Unlike a traditional course, no page here walks you through a screen, a database or a library. When a page points you somewhere, your AI helps you find it, reach it and understand it; every lab ends with three questions you work out with your AI and then explain to a partner without the screen. The known answers tell you whether what you found is right. The one-line prompts on the week pages are for you; nothing collects them.

Checkpoints are submitted at a lab's checkout.

Prerequisites — your setup is yours

Come to Session 1 with these working. Setting them up and fixing them is your responsibility, with your AI's help, and technical problems with your own setup are not grounds for an extension. In Session 1 your partner checks each one with you, and anything still failing must work by Session 2, when your desk is built. To start, ask any AI chat: "Walk me through installing Python 3.11 and [the agent you chose] on my [Windows or Mac] laptop, one step at a time."

  1. A laptop with a current Python 3 that runs a program from a terminal. Check: python --version (on Windows, if python is not found, try py --version).
  2. Your AI agents and Orca. Required: OpenAI's Codex CLI, Google Antigravity with its agy command line, and Orca, which runs your agents side by side on your work folder. Claude Code is optional. Install each from its own page. A browser chat is not a coding agent: it cannot create and run files in your folder. Two agents because having a second model check the first is this course's verification lesson applied to your own build.
  3. An API key with one model provider, created in the provider's developer console: for example OpenAI, Anthropic or Google AI Studio. Your desk calls a model through that key. A chat subscription does not include one.
  4. A public GitHub repository for your work. Nobody is invited: the instructor, and the teaching assistant if one is assigned, read it as anyone can. Put files there with git push, or on github.com with Add file → Upload files. One warning: the repository is public, so a key pushed there is a key stolen. Dragging a whole folder onto github.com bypasses .gitignore and would publish your .env.
  5. Accounts with the data Purdue licenses: LSEG Workspace, WRDS and S&P Capital IQ, each registered with your Purdue email as the data page describes. Register in Week 1: LSEG's approval takes days, and you first need the data in Week 3.

Keep every key out of GitHub: Lab 01 has your AI create a .env file with placeholders, tell you what to paste where, and exclude it with .gitignore. Check github.com afterward that .env is not there.

The data you use

Public filings and XBRL data from SEC EDGAR, LSEG Workspace and WRDS through Purdue, and whatever else your own code can reach. The data page is the contract: what the reader returns, the known answers, and the one rule.
