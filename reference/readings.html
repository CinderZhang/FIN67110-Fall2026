Readings — required and further, by week

The required readings are on each week's page; this page repeats them in one list and adds further reading. The test for a reading is whether it teaches, not where it lives: good Substack posts sit beside journal articles. Every link was checked on 2026-10-15. Papers behind a paywall open through the Purdue Libraries login.

Week 1 — ready to build

  • Francis, A., & Zhang, C. X. (2026). Can AI match professional analysts? Evidence from a multi-agent system. Finance Research Letters, 103, 110127. doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2026.110127, through the Purdue Libraries login.
  • Anthropic (2024). Building effective agents. anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents.
  • Karpathy, A. (2025). Software Is Changing (Again). Y Combinator AI Startup School. youtube.com/watch?v=LCEmiRjPEtQ.
  • Zhang, C. (2026). The Middle Is for the Machine. The Edges Are for You. cinderzhang.substack.com.
  • Karpathy, A. (2026, October 2). Post on reading what language models write, on X. x.com/karpathy/status/2105819303471976479. The source of "80% of the way to ASD-STE100".
  • ASD (2025). ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9. Request the free standard at asd-ste100.org. The writing rules are what "80% of the way to STE" keeps.
  • danyuchn, asd-ste100-skill: a skill for AI coding agents that applies those rules, with a linter. github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill. Install it in your agent if you want the rewrite in one request.
  • Further: Zhang, C. (2025, December 29). The DRIVER Best Practices Guide. cinderzhang.substack.com. The six stages, the loops, and prompting for learning and for problem-solving.
  • Further: Osmani, A. (2026, January 28). The 80% Problem in Agentic Coding. addyo.substack.com. Agents write most of the code; the last stretch is the hard part, and "comprehension debt" is what you owe on code you no longer understand. Your Reflect beats are the repayment.

Week 4 — the investor's desk, and the AI build-out

The value investor. Required: the first three. The rest are further reading, each with the reason it is on the list.

  • Graham, B. (1973). The Intelligent Investor, fourth revised edition, with commentary by J. Zweig (HarperBusiness, 2003). Chapter 8, the investor and market fluctuations (Mr. Market: a price is an offer, not a verdict); Chapter 14, stock selection for the defensive investor (tests a persona can score with numbers: size, a current ratio of at least two, a record of earnings and dividends, earnings growth, P/E at most 15, P/E times price-to-book at most 22.5); Chapter 20, margin of safety as the central concept of investment.
  • Buffett, W. E. (1984). The Superinvestors of Graham-and-Doddsville. Hermes, Columbia Business School, Fall 1984. Start here: the essay's record. The argument that value investors beat a market called efficient. Read it beside Fama and French.
  • Marks, H. (2011). The Most Important Thing: Uncommon Sense for the Thoughtful Investor. Columbia University Press. Chapter 2, understanding market efficiency and its limitations: when a value investor can expect a mispricing, and when not. His memos are free: oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos.
  • Further: Fama, E. F., & French, K. R. (2021). The value premium. Review of Asset Pricing Studies, 11(1), 105–121. SSRN. The value premium was much lower in the second half of 1963–2019, and too noisy to say it is gone. The evidence a value desk's claims are read against.
  • Further: Greenblatt, J. (2010). The Little Book That Still Beats the Market. Wiley. Two ratios, return on capital and earnings yield, ranked: the mechanical value screen a desk can code, and the baseline a guru persona must beat to be worth its calls.
  • Further: Munger, C. T. (2005). Poor Charlie's Almanack, ed. P. D. Kaufman; Stripe Press edition, 2023. The talk "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom" (1994): why Buffett moved from Graham's cheap stocks to good businesses at fair prices, the disagreement between two personas your desk may run. Also "The Psychology of Human Misjudgment".
  • Further: Graham, B., & Dodd, D. L. (2008). Security Analysis, sixth edition. McGraw-Hill. A reference shelf, not a week's reading. Read Seth Klarman's preface, "The Timeless Wisdom of Graham and Dodd".
  • Further: Klarman, S. A. (1991). Margin of Safety: Risk-Averse Value Investing Strategies for the Thoughtful Investor. HarperCollins. Out of print and never reprinted; ask the Purdue Libraries. His preface to Security Analysis (above) is the Klarman most students can reach.

  • Further: Greenblatt, J. (1997). You Can Be a Stock Market Genius. Simon & Schuster. Special situations: spinoffs, restructurings, merger securities, recapitalizations. The mispricing comes from forced selling, not from a cheap ratio, so it is a different desk from this week's; a spinoff does announce itself on EDGAR (Form 10), which a desk could watch.

  • Koijen, R., & Levy, I. Explaining Markets, the live benchmark. explainingmarkets.ai.

  • Rozenblit, L., & Keil, F. (2002). The misunderstood limits of folk science: an illusion of explanatory depth. Cognitive Science, 26(5), 521–562.
  • Van Nieuwerburgh, S. (2026). Financing the AI Buildout. Brookings Papers on Economic Activity, conference draft, September 2026. PDF.
  • Further: CNBC (2026, July 24). Bond market anxiety is growing over AI capex budgets. cnbc.com.
  • Further: Jain, R. (2026, July 28). The AI Capex Bubble: Bigger Than You Think, Funded by You Don't Know Who. Pinetree Macro, pinetreemacroresearch.substack.com. Private credit, off-balance-sheet debt and circular vendor deals; read it against the Brookings draft and decide which claims a filing can support.
  • Further: Breckinridge Capital Advisors (2026). The price of AI: how capex is rewriting tech balance sheets. breckinridge.com.
  • The filings behind the Week 4 table: Oracle's 10-Qs and 10-K, linked in the table, and the 2026-09-10 release.

Week 5 — the institution's desk

  • Any large mutual fund's summary prospectus, from the fund's own site: objective, principal strategies, fee table.
  • Sharpe, W. F. (1991). The arithmetic of active management. Financial Analysts Journal, 47(1), 7–9. JSTOR.
  • Bank for International Settlements (2026). Progress and peril. Annual Economic Report 2026, chapter I. bis.org.
  • Wang, Z. J., Zhang, H., & Zhang, X. (2020). Fire Sales and Impediments to Liquidity Provision in the Corporate Bond Market. Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, doi.org/10.1017/S0022109019000991, through the Purdue Libraries login. What a mandate's constraints do when bonds must be sold: funds with less cash, less liquid holdings and higher redemption risk provide less liquidity, and the ones that do earn it. The bond fund's side of the Week 5 application.
  • Further, the credit side of the Oracle case: Reuters (2026, October 15), Cost of AI default insurance spikes as data centre confidence wobbles, carried by investing.com: Oracle's five-year credit default swap at a record 288 basis points. CNBC (2026, February 2), Oracle's credit default swaps are plummeting as financing plan boosts investor confidence, cnbc.com: the same gauge eight months earlier, moving the other way. Bloomberg (2026, July 16), Oracle faces credit downgrade as AI spending outpaces cash flow, bloomberg.com (paywalled): the S&P Global cut to BBB-, one notch above junk, in July 2026.
