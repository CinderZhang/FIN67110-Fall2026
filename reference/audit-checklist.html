The report audit

A desk's report is audited against the filings the desk read, named on the report's first line: the latest 10-K, the latest 10-Q and the latest earnings release (the 8-K's Exhibit 99.1), with the XBRL company facts as the statements' data. Record where you found each number.

The core claims, fixed for everyone

Eight from the latest 10-K, three from the latest earnings release. Every desk states them, so any two desks can be compared.

# Claim Where it lives
1 Total revenue, latest fiscal year income statement; the revenue tag for that period
2 Revenue growth versus the prior year computed from the two years in the same filing
3 Operating income and operating margin income statement; margin = operating income ÷ total revenue
4 Net income attributable to the company income statement
5 Diluted earnings per share, GAAP per-share section
6 Return on average equity net income ÷ the average of this year's and last year's stockholders' equity, balance sheet
7 Debt to equity long-term debt including its current portion ÷ stockholders' equity, balance sheet
8 Free cash flow cash from operations − additions to property and equipment, cash flow statement
9 The latest quarter's revenue the earnings release, income statement
10 The quarter's growth versus the same quarter last year computed from the release's two columns
11 The quarter's diluted EPS, GAAP the release, per-share section

Then every other number in the Financial Analyst's part (peers, extra ratios, P/E), each with the same marks. Then two rows for the report itself: the template (the five parts present: Overview; Market Positioning and Growth Outlook; Valuation Insights; Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations; Investment Recommendation) and the recommendation (Buy, Hold or Sell names a number the report states, and the thesis uses numbers the audit marked correct).

How to mark a claim

  • Correct — the report's number matches the filing after rounding to what the report shows. A computed claim is correct if its inputs are in the filing and the arithmetic holds.
  • Wrong — it does not match. Write the filing's number beside it.
  • Unsupported — the report gives a number the filings do not contain: a guess, a different period, or a different definition presented as this one.
  • Missing — the report does not state the claim. A non-GAAP EPS figure, even labeled, does not count as claim 5 or 11; if the report states only that, mark the claim missing.
  • Not in filings — the filings do not state the claim and the report gives no number for it. This counts for nobody. A number the report invents for such a claim is unsupported.

Report two counts: errors (wrong plus unsupported) and missing. A desk can look accurate by saying less, so a comparison of two reports always shows both counts.

Start here: the company's filings on SEC EDGAR (the company search) and its XBRL company facts (data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json, ten digits). For Costco: the 10-K filing list, the 8-K filing list (the latest earnings release is the newest 8-K that reports Item 2.02), and the company facts.

Definitions are the common trap. Where a filing offers two (gross margin on net sales or on total revenue, equity at year end or on average), the claim's definition is the one in the table above, or, where the table names none, the one the company itself uses; the Week 2 page works the gross-margin case.
