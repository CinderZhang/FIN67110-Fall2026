Week 1 — Ready to Build

Mission. Get your setup working with a partner watching, learn what a model, a harness and an agent are, and meet the desk you build next week: the four-role desk of a published paper, reading a company's filings and writing an equity research report.

Why this matters

In earnings season a junior analyst at a bank or fund covers a dozen companies. The night a company reports, the junior reads the release against the 10-K and 10-Q, pulls the numbers everyone will quote, and drafts the report before the morning meeting. Get one number wrong and a portfolio manager trades on it. Desks now hand that first draft to AI. Your job, here and on the desk, is to build the AI that drafts and to be the reason the draft can be trusted. That starts with a laptop that runs what you tell your agent to build.

Learning partners

Each session opens with a paired review. Partners change freely; your partner is your first line of support. Verify each other's setup, defend your numbers aloud, and check before asking the room. Professional analysts explain their work every day; you start now.

You arrive with

The prerequisites on the grading page working, or the exact error text of the one that is not.

Outcomes

  • Show a partner that your agents run in your folder, create a file, and read a key from .env that never reaches your public repository.
  • Say what a model, a harness and an agent are, and which boxes of Figure 1 are which.
  • Describe the four roles in Francis & Zhang's Figure 1, the tools each uses, and the five parts of the report they produce.

Session 1 — Lab 01: Ready to build.

Read and watch this week

  • Francis, A., & Zhang, C. X. (2026). Can AI match professional analysts? Evidence from a multi-agent system. Finance Research Letters, 103, 110127. doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2026.110127, through the Purdue Libraries login. Read the introduction, Section 3.1 (the architecture) and Section 4 (results), about 30 minutes.
  • Anthropic (2024). Building effective agents. Read to the end of "Workflow: prompt chaining", about 15 minutes. Two definitions to keep: a workflow runs model calls along a path the code fixes; an agent lets the model choose its own steps and tools.
  • Karpathy, A. (2025). Software Is Changing (Again), Y Combinator. Watch for two ideas: a prompt is a program, and every AI tool has an autonomy slider that you set.
  • Zhang, C. (2026). The Middle Is for the Machine. The Edges Are for You. The course method, about 15 minutes.
  • Karpathy, A. (2026). His post on reading what models write, on X, about 3 minutes. Ask your model to explain things in ASD-STE100, or "80% of the way to ASD-STE100" because the full standard is strict; then diagrams, web pages and explainer videos, each, he says, better than the last. You put his first tip in your agents' instructions in Lab 01.

Make your agents write so you can check them

Aircraft maintenance manuals are written in a controlled English, ASD-STE100, because the technician on the tarmac cannot phone the author: one meaning per word, one instruction per sentence, active voice, short sentences, and a hedge kept as a hedge. Karpathy's tip turns it around: ask your model to write to you that way, "80% of the way" because the full standard is strict. Most of your work this term is reading what agents wrote and deciding whether to trust it, and a short sentence with one claim is a sentence you can check. It runs the other way too: a role's instructions are read by a model that cannot ask what you meant. One rule comes with it: a rewrite keeps every number, condition and "may"; "may have" never becomes "did".

Four documents you need before Lab 02

A 10-K is a company's annual report to the SEC, with the audited statements, the business description and management's discussion; a 10-Q is the quarterly version. An 8-K is filed when something material happens; the earnings release is attached to it as Exhibit 99.1. And XBRL is the tagged data behind the statements, which EDGAR serves as a file per company, so your code can read a number with its tag, period and filing instead of scraping a page. A User-Agent is the name and email EDGAR requires from every program that downloads any of them; yours goes in .env in Lab 01.

Model, harness, agent

Word What it is In your desk
Model A function: text in, text out. It remembers nothing between calls and cannot open a file. Each role is one call to it; Thursday of Week 2
Harness The program around the model: the loop, the tools it may call, the memory of the task, and the checks on what comes back Your desk: the filings tools, the calculator, the Manager's loop
Agent A model and a harness given a goal The desk, and the coding agent that builds it

The coding agent on your laptop is an agent whose tools are your files and your terminal. The desk you build next week is an agent whose tools are EDGAR's filings and data, a calculator, and the model. Same shape, different tools. When something goes wrong, the first question is always which of the two failed: the model said something false, or the harness fed it the wrong thing and did not check.

The paper in five numbers

The authors built a four-agent desk on one model and had it write reports on 50 U.S. companies for the third quarter of 2024. Blinded readers compared each with the professional report from Value Line.

Who judged How many comparisons Preferred the AI report
Four AI judges 200 71%, ranging from 48% to 96% by judge
MSF students 45 51%
General public 194 77%

Two more results matter for this course. First, neither the AI's buy recommendations nor Value Line's beat simply holding all 50 companies equally. Second, nobody in the study checked whether the numbers inside the reports were right. The paper says so in its conclusion. That gap is your term.

Figure 1, the desk you will build

flowchart TD
    M[Strategy Manager<br/>assigns tasks as JSON, checks the five parts,<br/>sends a part back once if not] --> R[Research Analyst<br/>tools: the filings reader<br/>business model, position, catalysts, recent developments]
    M --> F[Financial Analyst<br/>tools: the filings reader, the XBRL facts, a calculator<br/>statements, ratios, growth, peers, each with its source]
    M --> A[Investment Advisor<br/>no tools: the two roles' outputs<br/>thesis, Buy / Hold / Sell, the deciding number]
    R --> M
    F --> M
    A --> M
    M --> N[The report, five parts]

Each box is one call to a model with its own instructions. The Strategy Manager is not a smarter model. It is a loop with a checklist: send a task as JSON, read what comes back, send it back once if a part is missing, then compile the five parts (Overview; Market Positioning and Growth Outlook; Valuation Insights; Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations; Investment Recommendation). That loop, the instructions, and the tools each role may use are the harness. They are what turn a model into a desk.

Worked example — what the Financial Analyst must get right

Open Costco's latest earnings release: on Costco's 8-K filing list, the newest 8-K that reports Item 2.02, then its Exhibit 99.1. The instructor opens the same page on the screen. The quarter's claims, and where each lives in the release:

Claim Where it lives in the release
Total revenue the income statement, the quarter's column
Growth versus the same quarter last year this quarter's total revenue ÷ the same quarter last year − 1, two columns of one table
Operating income the income statement
Net income the income statement, attributable to Costco
Diluted earnings per share the per-share lines, GAAP
Cash and cash equivalents the balance sheet
Diluted shares the shares used in the per-share calculation, diluted

A desk that copies the GAAP diluted EPS from its line is right. A desk that adds back an item the release discusses, or reports an adjusted EPS as the GAAP figure, has made a number the release's line never states: when a release says an item added to EPS, the item is already inside the GAAP figure. The year, read from the 10-K, is the Week 2 page's worked example. Next week you see which kind your desk is.
