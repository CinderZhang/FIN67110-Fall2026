Why finance is a special field for AI

  • Finance has low signal and high noise.
  • The data is moving and not stationary.
  • Verification is hard, so reinforcement learning is hard.
  • Human subtle wisdom can pay off for the few who do it better.
  • The magic of accumulated return and compounding turns a small edge into a large one.
  • $1 million at 8% a year becomes about $10 million in 30 years.
  • At 10%, it becomes about $17 million. Two points of edge are worth about $7 million.

Quant firms become AI labs

  • Market makers like Jane Street, XTX Markets, Citadel Securities and Hudson River Trading earn small spreads.
  • Quant trading moved from a speed era (about 2000 to 2010) to a model era.
  • Almost every quant firm is now becoming an AI lab.
  • Jane Street signed multi-year compute contracts of about $19 billion.
  • XTX Markets held about 25,000 GPUs with about 200 people.
  • These firms do not invest on FOMO; they invest after it makes money.

Infra beats data, data beats the model

  • I rank a firm's assets: infrastructure first, data second, and the model last.
  • Models converge in the AI era, and exchanges send everyone the same feed.
  • About 70% of the data is the same for every firm.
  • One moat is a firm's own trading history: which positions made money, and why.
  • The other moat is execution and risk infrastructure.
  • This infrastructure is not easy to vibe-code with Claude.

Risk before return

  • If you lose 50%, you need a 100% gain to get back.
  • A forecast is not a profit.
  • Risk control, portfolio limits, market impact and execution sit between a forecast and a profit.
  • A large order tells the market what you want, and the price moves against you.
  • A good quant firm first survives a long time.
  • The firm respects risk and respects discipline.

Crowding comes from scarce information

  • Firms that chase the same scarce signal learn similar models with any tool.
  • When signals are too alike, firms fail together in a stress event.
  • Examples include the 2007 Quant Quake and a stampede in China in early 2024.
  • In 2007, Jim Simons cut positions while his model said to hold.
  • Firms escape crowding by changing their objectives and finding orthogonal signals.
  • Orthogonal signals work well alone and differ from what the firm already runs.

Where judgment goes in a quant firm

  • Human judgment goes into building the model, not into overriding it.
  • A loss inside the strategy's statistical range is normal, so do not intervene.
  • You act when losses leave that range, from overfitting or market change.
  • Tuning parameters on an existing model rarely adds money.
  • A new idea that differs from the current model is the hard part.
  • An AI agent copies ten ideas easily, but struggles to produce one different idea.

From quant to discretionary: a spectrum

  • No clear line separates quant and discretionary investing.
  • In quant investing, AI collects information, finds the signal and makes the decision.
  • In discretionary investing, AI does the front end and the person keeps the decision.
  • The best discretionary investors are more machine than the machine, calm and objective.
  • Their edge is undigitized information from years in an industry and talks with people.

The limits of judgment

  • People are fooled by stories too, and see change where there is only noise.
  • Judgment counts when you write it down before the outcome and check it after.
  • Once you write a check, the machine runs it every time.
  • Your value moves to the next trap, not the last one.
  • Judgment goes into the rules before the run.
  • Discipline keeps fear out during the run.

Part 2: AI-enabled finance professionals

  • In low-frequency investing, people use AI to help them decide.
  • The platforms on the next slides build AI for the person who keeps the decision.
  • AI tools make investors better informed.
  • A signal sold to many users stops working.
  • The tools give everyone the same first read, so your edge is what you do next.

MCP: Data as Tools Agents Call

  • Anthropic first developed the Model Context Protocol, an open standard called USB-C for AI.
  • MCP turns a data set into tools an AI agent can call.
  • It keeps the user's licenses and permissions.
  • LSEG, FactSet, Bloomberg, Capital IQ, Multiples and Robinhood all offer MCP connections.

Cheaper, Focused Tools

  • Tegus, now part of AlphaSense, holds about 300,000 expert-call transcripts.
  • Users ask questions across the Tegus transcripts.
  • Multiples provides public comps and deal multiples, with an MCP server for Claude and ChatGPT.
  • Multiples costs an estimated $240 to $500 a month.
  • Each tool does one job well, at a fraction of a terminal seat.

Retail agents: who approves the trade

  • Cortex explains in plain English why a stock or a portfolio moves.
  • Robinhood Agents run loops that watch the market and can trade around the clock.
  • Each agent trades only in its own sandboxed account.
  • Trade approval is on by default: the user confirms each order.
  • The user can turn approval off.
  • Robinhood does not supervise the agents; the user owns the losses.

  • Consolidate and anonymize the data before you call an AI tool.

What can go wrong

  • A model can state a wrong number with full confidence.
  • A correct number can still support a wrong conclusion.
  • A document can hide an instruction addressed to the model.
  • Crowding: teams on the same models and data can fail together.
  • Overfitting makes a strong backtest fail live.

Audit one claim

  1. The release reports both adjusted EPS and GAAP EPS.
  2. The agent drafts "EPS grew" and does not say which EPS.
  3. A verifier recomputes both figures from the filing and flags the claim.
  4. A hook blocks any figure without a source.
  5. You decide which measure belongs in the valuation, and you write down why.
  6. You name the evidence that would change your mind.

Buy, build on top, or build for others

  • One discipline offers two paths.
  • An employee buys data, terminals, and assistants, then builds workflows, checks, and firm knowledge on top.
  • An entrepreneur builds the layer that others buy.
  • Before you trust any AI, ask whether each number traces to the filing.
  • Ask whether something recomputes the number, and ask who answers for the view.

If you want to build a company

  • With AI tools, a small team can now do what once took a big team.
  • Big firms skip niches too small to scale; small players can live there, such as prediction markets.
  • A business can inform investors, or gather what AI cannot reach: surveys and AI-driven polls.
  • Selling a signal cannot be a business, because a sold signal stops working.
  • Tegus and Multiples began by unbundling the terminal.
  • Venture capital wants fast growth, and fast growth without risk control loses a trading fund everything.

Taste in the AI Era

  • Taste in AI work comes down to two habits.
  • First, choose where to spend effort: go for the fundamental problem, not the small tweak.
  • Second, be able to audit a conclusion.
  • When Claude writes code for five minutes, you may review it for two hours.
  • If you stop reviewing AI output for one moment, you never catch up.
  • As AI gets better, the gap grows between people with good taste and others.

What Education Must Change

  • AI now does the junior work that once taught new analysts.
  • The old ladder breaks because firms cannot train people the old way.
  • I think apprenticeship will replace the current lecture model in finance training.
  • AI explains the concepts, while a master reviews your judgment.
  • You learn by building beside someone better, with feedback on every piece.
  • Good masters are scarce, so apprenticeship is hard to scale.

The edge is trust

  • Whether you buy or build, your edge is something that others can run and trust.
  • If a stranger cannot run your work, nobody can adopt it.
  • Build tools that prove their own work.
